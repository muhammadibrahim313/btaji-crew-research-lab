BTAJI CREW RESEARCH LAB — SESSION 3
THE GAP VALIDATION PROMPT   (reusable template + worked examples)
================================================================

WHAT THIS IS
The second prompt. After Prompt 1 (the sweep) helps you spot a candidate gap, this
one checks, honestly, whether that gap is already filled, BEFORE you spend months on
it. It acts as a skeptical reviewer whose job is to try to kill your idea.

HOW TO USE IT
1. Write your candidate gap as one or two sentences, in the shape
   "Everyone did X, but nobody did Y, because of Z".
2. Fill in the [BRACKETS] in PART 1. (PART 2 and PART 3 show filled examples.)
3. Run it FRESH, in a new chat, ideally on a DIFFERENT tool than the one that first
   suggested the gap, so the model is not just defending its own earlier answer.
4. Read the verdict: OPEN, PARTIALLY taken, or already DONE (see the bottom).
5. GOLDEN RULE stays the same: only trust papers with a working link you can open.


================================================================
PART 1 — THE PROMPT   (fill in the [BRACKETS], then copy everything between the lines)
================================================================
----------------------------------------------------------------

You are a SKEPTICAL, SENIOR PEER REVIEWER. I have a candidate research gap and I am
about to spend months on it. Your job is NOT to encourage me. It is to try hard to
PROVE the gap is already filled, before I commit. Assume it has probably been done,
and hunt for the paper that did it.

MY CANDIDATE GAP:
[WRITE YOUR GAP in one or two sentences, in the shape:
 "Everyone did X, but nobody did Y, because of Z"]

FIELD:
[the field, plus its main benchmark and metric if there is one, so you know where to look]

STEPS:
1. Search hard across arXiv, Papers With Code, OpenReview, Semantic Scholar, Google
   Scholar, ACL Anthology or the venues for this field, plus competition and challenge
   write-ups and MSc/PhD theses, for any paper that already does this, or comes close.
2. Look SPECIFICALLY for near-misses that only PARTIALLY close it. A naive gap looks
   uninformed if it misses these. Find the CURRENT state, not the state from two years ago.
3. Label each close paper:
   - DIRECT: it already does essentially my study. My gap is not open.
   - PARTIAL: it does some of it. Name the exact piece it leaves undone.
   - ADJACENT: same area, but does not address my gap.
4. Give an honest verdict.

EVIDENCE STANDARD (this stops you inventing a convenient match): any paper you cite as
DIRECT or PARTIAL must have BOTH a resolvable link AND a short direct quote or the exact
figure/table number where the result appears. If you cannot produce that, list it as a
"lead to check", not as evidence. Do not let a lead change the verdict.

RETURN, in this order:
- VERDICT: one of "OPEN", "PARTIALLY taken", or "already DONE".
- EVIDENCE TABLE:
  Paper | Month/Year | Link (resolvable) | Closeness (DIRECT/PARTIAL/ADJACENT) | Quote or figure/table ref | What they did vs what I want
- THE CLOSEST PAPER: 3 lines. Exactly what it does, and precisely how my study would
  still differ (or not).
- IF OPEN or PARTIALLY taken: rewrite my gap tighter as
  "Everyone did X, but nobody did Y, because Z", narrowed to survive the near-misses you found.
- IF already DONE: name the paper that closes it, and suggest one nearby angle still open.

HARD RULES: only cite real papers whose page you actually opened this session; never
invent a paper to fill the table; if you find nothing, say "I found no direct work on
this", which is a valid and useful answer. Be honest, not encouraging. Do not pad. If,
after genuinely trying to kill this gap, you cannot find a paper that does it, say so
clearly. That is your validated gap.

----------------------------------------------------------------
(end of prompt)

================================================================
READING THE VERDICT
================================================================
OPEN               Nobody did it. Go.
PARTIALLY taken    Someone did part of it. Narrow your gap to the exact piece they left,
                   then you are ready.
already DONE       It exists. Pick your next candidate from the sweep and repeat. This is
                   normal. It often takes two or three tries.

A rejected gap is not a failure. It just saved you three months.
